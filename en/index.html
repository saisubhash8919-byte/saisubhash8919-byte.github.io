Common illness

Common cold & cough

Most colds are viral and settle on their own within 7–10 days. Comfort care helps you rest and recover.

Helpful at home

  • Rest and drink warm fluids — water, soups, herbal tea.
  • Breathe steam from a bowl of hot water or a warm shower to ease congestion (keep children away from hot water).
  • Gargle with warm salt water for a scratchy throat.
  • Honey in warm water can soothe a cough in adults and children over 1 year.
  • Wash hands often and cover coughs to protect others.

Best to avoid

  • Antibiotics without a doctor's advice — they do not work on viral colds.
  • Giving honey to babies under 12 months.
  • Smoking or smoky rooms, which slow recovery.

See a doctor if

  • Difficulty breathing, wheezing or chest pain.
  • High fever lasting more than 3 days.
  • Symptoms lasting beyond 10 days or getting worse after improving.
  • Coughing up blood.

Health tips

Small habits, big difference

Simple, evidence-informed tips for everyday health. A new tip every day — and filters to find what you need.

Tip of the day

  • Hydration

    Start with a glass of water

    Drink a glass of water after you wake up. Keep a bottle in sight through the day — visible water gets drunk.

  • Hydration

    Check the colour

    Pale-straw urine usually means you're well hydrated. Dark yellow is a cue to drink more, especially in hot weather.

  • Hydration

    Eat your water

    Cucumber, watermelon, oranges, buttermilk and soups all add to your daily fluid intake.

  • Sleep

    Keep a fixed wake-up time

    Waking at the same time every day — weekends included — steadies your body clock more than anything else.

  • Sleep

    Screens off 30–60 minutes before bed

    Bright screens delay sleepiness. Swap the phone for reading, stretching or quiet music.

  • Sleep

    Cool, dark, quiet

    A cool, dark room helps you fall and stay asleep. Most adults need 7–9 hours a night.

  • Movement

    Aim for 150 minutes a week

    Adults benefit from at least 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly — about 30 minutes, 5 days a week. Brisk walking counts.

  • Movement

    Break up sitting

    Stand up, stretch or walk for 2–3 minutes every 30–60 minutes of sitting.

  • Movement

    Two days of strength

    Muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week supports bones, balance and blood sugar. Body-weight exercises work.

  • Nutrition

    Half a plate of vegetables

    Fill half your plate with vegetables and fruit, a quarter with whole grains and a quarter with protein like dal, eggs, fish or paneer.

  • Nutrition

    Watch hidden salt and sugar

    Pickles, papad, namkeen, sauces and packaged drinks carry a lot of salt or sugar. Check labels.

  • Nutrition

    Don't skip breakfast

    A balanced breakfast with protein and fibre keeps energy steady and reduces mid-morning cravings.

  • Mind

    Box breathing

    Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat four times to calm a racing mind.

  • Mind

    Get daylight early

    10–15 minutes of morning daylight lifts mood and helps sleep later that night.

  • Mind

    Talk it out

    Sharing worries with someone you trust lightens the load. If low mood lasts more than two weeks, speak to a professional.

  • Prevention

    Wash hands for 20 seconds

    Soap and water before eating and after the toilet is one of the most effective ways to prevent infections.

  • Prevention

    Know your numbers

    Check blood pressure, blood sugar and weight at least once a year after age 30, or as your doctor advises.

  • Prevention

    Keep vaccinations current

    Ask your doctor which vaccines are due for you — including tetanus boosters and seasonal flu vaccination.

        Health education

        Understand your health, clearly

        Plain-language explanations of the health concepts that matter most in daily life.

        Understanding vital signs

        Vital signs are simple measurements that show how your body is working. Typical adult resting ranges are below — your own normal may differ.

        Heart rate
        About 60–100 beats per minute at rest for most adults. Fit people may be lower.
        Body temperature
        Around 36.1–37.2°C (97–99°F). 38°C (100.4°F) or above is generally a fever.
        Blood pressure
        Below 120/80 mmHg is considered normal. Repeated readings of 130/80 or above deserve a doctor's review.
        Oxygen saturation (SpO₂)
        Usually 95–100% on a pulse oximeter. Persistent readings below 94%, or any drop with breathlessness, need medical attention.
        Breathing rate
        About 12–20 breaths per minute at rest.

        One reading is a snapshot. Trends over days, and how you feel, matter more.

        2. Crowdfunding platforms

        Donation-based medical crowdfunding is legal in India. Compare fees and how each platform verifies campaigns.

        Ketto Milaap ImpactGuru

        Before you donate

        1. Call the hospital on its official number and confirm the patient and the estimate.
        2. Pay only through the platform or to the hospital's account — never to a personal UPI ID or WhatsApp number.
        3. Look for medical documents, the hospital's name and a doctor's details on the campaign.
        4. For an NGO, check its registration (NGO Darpan) and 80G certificate.
        5. Be careful if you are pressured to give right now or to keep it secret.
        6. Search the patient's name and photo — fake campaigns copy real stories.

        If you are raising money

        • Use a platform that verifies documents with the hospital.
        • Never share an OTP, PIN or full Aadhaar/bank details in messages.
        • Keep all receipts and give donors regular updates.
        • Money raised by an individual may be taxable, and foreign donations need FCRA permission — ask an accountant.

        Cheated by a fundraiser?

        Call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) or report at cybercrime.gov.in.

        Common illness

        Fever in adults

        Fever is the body's response to infection. A temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or above is generally considered a fever.

        Helpful at home

        • Rest and drink plenty of fluids; oral rehydration solution (ORS) helps if you are sweating a lot.

        • Wear light clothing and keep the room comfortably cool.

        • Sponge with lukewarm (not cold) water for comfort.

        See a doctor if

        • Temperature of 39.4°C (103°F) or higher, or fever lasting over 3 days.

        • Stiff neck, severe headache, confusion or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed — seek emergency care.

        Designed to be safe and honest

        Trust comes from being clear about what Sanjeevani is — and what it isn't.

        • Helps you build and track healthy daily habits

        What it never does

        • Diagnose diseases or medical conditions
        • Prescribe medicines or suggest doses
